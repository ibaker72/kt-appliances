# Putting appliances on the KT Appliances website

This is for whoever runs the floor. You do not need a developer, and you do not need
a computer — everything here works from a phone in the warehouse.

**Where you do it:** `https://kt-appliances.com/admin`
**How you get in:** the admin password. It is not the same as your email password. If
nobody has given you one, see "Who has the password" at the bottom.

---

## Add an appliance

1. Go to **`/admin/inventory`** and tap **Add Appliance**.
2. Fill in the five things the form insists on:
   - **Brand** — Samsung, Whirlpool, LG, GE…
   - **Category** — refrigerators, washers, dryers, sets, ranges, dishwashers, other.
   - **Product name** — what it is and how big. *"28 cu. ft. French Door Refrigerator"*.
     Do not repeat the brand here; the site puts it in front automatically.
   - **KT price** — whole dollars. `899`, not `899.00`.
   - **Condition** — usually Scratch & Dent.
3. Tap **Create Appliance**. The listing exists now, and you land on its page.
4. **Add photos** (next section). Do this before you leave the page.
5. Scroll to **6 · Publish**, set **Stock status** to *Available*, make sure
   **Published** is ticked, and tap **Save Changes**.

It is on the website within a couple of minutes. You do not have to do anything else,
and nobody has to "push it live".

### What is worth filling in, and what is not

Everything past the five required fields is optional, and you can come back later. In
order of what actually sells appliances:

| Field | Why it earns its place |
| --- | --- |
| **Cosmetic damage** | The single biggest trust factor. Say where the dent is, how big, and whether it shows once installed. People buy scratch & dent *because* it is honest. |
| **Model number** | People search Google for the model number. This is how they find you. |
| **What you tested** | "Cooling, freezer and ice maker tested." Turns a gamble into a purchase. |
| **Dimensions** | Prevents the wasted delivery where it does not fit through the door. |
| **Fuel type** | On ranges and dryers this is not optional in practice — gas and electric are not interchangeable. |
| **Retail comparison** | Only if you can actually verify it for *that* model. See the warning below. |

### The retail comparison price

Leave it blank unless you have checked the real retail price for that exact model. The
site refuses to show a comparison that is not higher than your price, and it never
invents one. A made-up "was $1,599" is the fastest way to look like every other fake
appliance site.

## Add photos

On the appliance's page, under **Photos**:

1. Tap **Add photos**. Your phone offers the camera or the photo library — either is fine.
2. Pick up to **8 at a time**. Photos straight off a phone are fine; they get shrunk
   automatically before they upload, so warehouse wifi is not a problem.
3. You see thumbnails of what you picked, with the size. Tap **Upload photos**.

**What to shoot, in this order:**

1. The front of the unit, straight on, whole appliance in frame.
2. **The damage.** Close enough to see it clearly. This is the photo that sells it.
3. The inside — shelves, drum, racks.
4. The model/serial plate if it is easy to reach.

### Choosing the main photo

The first photo is what shows on the category pages, in Google, and when someone shares
the link. To change it, tap the **star** on the photo you want. Use the arrows to
reorder, and the bin to delete one.

### Marking where the damage is

Under the photos there is a damage map. Tap the spot on the photo where the mark is and
describe it — *"Dent, lower left door — 2 inches"*. Those markers appear on the customer's
photo. Leave it empty if there is nothing to mark; the site shows nothing rather than
claiming the unit is unmarked.

## Change a price

1. `/admin/inventory` → tap the appliance.
2. Change **KT price**.
3. **Save Changes**.

The website shows the new price within about two minutes. **The web address does not
change**, so any link you already texted or posted still works.

---

## Replace photos

Same page. Delete the ones you do not want (bin icon, it asks first), upload the new
ones, star the one that should lead.

## Mark an appliance sold

**This is the one to get in the habit of.** From `/admin/inventory`, tap the **tag icon**
on the row — or **Mark sold** on the appliance's own page.

What happens:

- It disappears from the shop-able inventory immediately, so nobody calls about a unit
  that is gone.
- Its page **stays up** and shows **SOLD**, with links to similar appliances. The old
  Facebook post still works and sends the visitor to something you do have.
- The "call about this appliance" buttons come off, so nobody is invited to ask about it.

If you sell something and forget to mark it, the only cost is a phone call you have to
apologise for. It takes one tap — do it when the truck leaves.

## Take something off the site — Archive

**Archive** (the box icon) hides an appliance from the website completely and keeps
everything: photos, price, description, and its web address. Use it when a unit goes
back to the supplier, is pulled for repair, or was listed by mistake.

**Restore** (the box-with-arrow icon) puts it straight back at the same address.

### Archive vs Sold vs Delete

| You want to… | Use |
| --- | --- |
| It sold | **Mark sold** — page stays up, cross-sells, keeps your Google ranking |
| Off the site for now, might come back | **Archive** — reversible, nothing lost |
| Get rid of it permanently | **Delete** — see below |

**Delete is permanent.** It destroys the record and the photographs of that exact unit,
including the damage shots. There is no undo and no copy anywhere else. The site makes
you type the word DELETE to confirm, on purpose. In normal use you should almost never
need it — Archive does what you want.

## Feature something on the homepage

The **star** icon on a row puts an appliance in the featured position. Use it for a
handful at a time — the point is that they stand out.

---

## Draft an appliance before it is ready

Set **Stock status** to *Draft*, or untick **Published**. Either keeps it off the site
entirely while you finish photographing it. Nothing in draft is ever visible to a
customer or to Google.

**Duplicate as draft** copies an appliance you are about to list again — handy for a
second identical unit. It comes in as a draft with no SKU, and its damage map is
deliberately empty, because the second unit's dents are its own.

## Finding things

The search box at the top of `/admin/inventory` covers brand, model number, SKU and the
web address. The dropdowns filter by status (available / reserved / sold / draft) and by
whether something is live or archived.

---

## Customer enquiries

**`/admin/leads`** is every enquiry from the website, newest first. Each one shows the
name, the phone number as a tap-to-call button, and — when they asked from an appliance
page — **which appliance they were looking at**.

Set a status as you work them: New → Contacted → Quoted → Won or Lost. Mark obvious
junk as Spam.

You also get an email for every enquiry, with the same details and a link to the listing.

---

## Things that will bite you

- **A price with cents.** Type `899`, not `899.00`. The form strips it anyway, but get
  in the habit.
- **A retail comparison lower than your price.** The form refuses it. That is intentional.
- **Forgetting to publish.** A saved appliance with Published unticked is invisible.
  If you cannot find something on the website, check that first.
- **Photos of the box instead of the unit.** People are buying the dent, not the carton.
- **Leaving sold units live.** One tap. Every day.

## Who has the password

The admin password lives in the hosting settings, not in a file anyone can read, and it
can be changed at any time without touching the website. If someone leaves the business,
