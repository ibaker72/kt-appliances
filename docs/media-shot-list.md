# KT Appliances — photo & video shot list

This is everything the website needs. You can shoot all of it with your phone
in about an hour at the warehouse. Nothing needs special equipment.

**Before you start, set your phone up once:**

- Use the normal camera app. **No portrait mode, no filters, no cropping.**
- Hold the phone **sideways (landscape)** for every shot unless a shot below
  says otherwise.
- If your phone asks about format, HEIC or JPEG are both fine — send the
  **original files** (AirDrop, Google Drive, or email at "actual size").
  Don't send screenshots or anything a messaging app has compressed.
- Wipe the camera lens with your shirt first. It sounds silly; it's the #1
  cause of hazy photos.
- **Light:** open the bay door and shoot with daylight coming in whenever you
  can. The overhead fluorescents alone make everything look green and flat.
  If a shot looks orange or green on your screen, it'll look worse on the site.
- Keep every original. We resize and compress on our end — never you.

Shots are listed most-important first. If you only do one thing today, do
**Shot 1**.

---

## 1. The warehouse walk (video) — the single most valuable asset

**What:** A 10–15 second video walking slowly down your main aisle, with
appliances visible on both sides.

- Phone sideways (landscape).
- Hold the phone at chest height with both hands, elbows tucked against your
  body — that's what keeps it steady.
- Walk slower than feels natural. Half your normal pace.
- Don't talk, don't zoom, don't pan side to side. Just walk straight.
- Shoot it three times and send all three takes. One will be steadier than
  the others.
- Best light: bay door open, mid-morning or mid-afternoon.

**Where it goes:** It plays silently behind the big headline at the top of the
homepage — the first thing every visitor sees. This one clip does more for the
site than every other shot combined.

## 2. The floor, wide (photo)

**What:** Stand just inside the customer door and photograph the whole floor,
as many units in frame as possible.

- Landscape. Step back until the frame feels *full* of appliances.
- Hold the phone level — the shelf lines and appliance tops should be straight,
  not tilted.

**Where it goes:** Backup for the homepage top slot, and the picture shown when
the inventory page is between arrivals ("call us to ask what's in").

## 3. Delivery (photo)

**What:** The truck loaded with a unit visible, or two people carrying a unit
on a dolly. Whichever happens first — shoot it when it's really happening,
don't stage an empty truck.

- Landscape, outdoors, whole truck or both people fully in frame.

**Where it goes:** The "Delivery, installation and haul-away" slide on the
homepage, and the delivery page — which right now is a wall of text.

## 4. One photo per category — 7 shots

These replace the grey line drawings on the homepage category tiles.

**The same recipe for all seven:**

- Pick your best-looking unit in that category. It's fine if it has a dent —
  that's the business — but pick one that's clean and wiped down.
- Roll it out so nothing else is directly behind it if you can.
- Stand directly in front of it. Squat so the camera is at the middle of the
  unit, not looking down at it.
- Landscape. The unit should fill most of the frame with a little room on
  each side.
- Doors closed. Wipe fingerprints off stainless fronts — they show badly.

**The seven:**

1. **Refrigerator** — your best fridge, ideally stainless.
2. **Washer** — front-load if you have one, they photograph better.
3. **Dryer** — same recipe.
4. **Washer & dryer set** — the matched pair side by side, touching, fronts
   aligned. Wider shot, both fully in frame.
5. **Range / oven** — straight on, burners clean.
6. **Dishwasher** — front on, door closed.
7. **Other** — whatever represents that shelf best: a chest freezer, or a row
   of microwaves.

**Where they go:** The seven "Shop by category" tiles on the homepage — the row
people use to start shopping.

## 5. The building (3 photos)

1. **From the street:** the whole building with your signage readable. Cross
   the street or back up until sign and entrance are both in frame. Landscape.
   Daytime, sun behind you if possible.
2. **The entrance:** the customer door, close enough that posted hours are
   readable in the photo. This one can be vertical (portrait) — it fits a
   tall slot.
3. **Inside, two angles:** stand in one back corner and shoot toward the door;
   then from the opposite corner. Landscape.

**Where they go:** The "visit the warehouse" box with the map and hours, the
About page, and eventually the image that shows when someone shares the site
link in a text or on Facebook. This is the "we are a real place you can drive
to" evidence — the site currently has none.

## 6. People (2 photos)

1. **You (and family/team) on the floor.** Standing among the appliances, not
   against a wall. Look at the camera, relaxed — this isn't a passport photo.
   Landscape, waist-up or full body.
2. **Someone testing a unit.** Plugged in, panel lit or drum turning, person's
   hands on it. Get the lit display in frame — that's the proof.

**Where they go:** The About page and the "why buy from us" section. The logo
says *family owned* — the site should show the family. And "every unit tested"
is just words until there's a photo of it happening.

## 7. Damage close-ups (4–6 photos) — the trust builders

This feels backwards, but photos of dents are the most trust-building images
on the whole site. Buyers' one question is "how bad is the damage really?" —
showing typical examples answers it before they ask.

**What:** Close-ups of typical cosmetic damage on units you have now:

- A dented door corner
- A scuffed or dented side panel
- A scratch on a front panel
- Anything else you'd point out to a customer on the floor

**How:**

- About arm's length away — close enough to see it clearly, far enough that
  you can tell what part of the appliance it's on.
- Angle the phone slightly so light rakes across the dent — straight-on with
  flash makes dents invisible. **Flash off.**
- These can be portrait or landscape, whatever frames the damage best.
- For each one, text me a note like "dent, lower left door corner, about
  2 inches" — those words go under the photo.

**Where they go:** The "what does scratch & dent mean" guide, and later,
per-unit damage photos on each listing.

**Sending them:** originals at full size, all takes, no cropping or filters.
Any modern phone's camera is more than sharp enough — resolution is never the
problem; compression from messaging apps is. If in doubt, Google Drive or
AirDrop.

One more thing: location data. Photos taken away from the warehouse (like the
delivery shot) can carry the GPS location of wherever you're standing. We strip
that from every photo before it goes on the site — you don't need to do
anything, just know it's handled.
